# Real-camera Lighting Lab experiment (do this yourself, ~45 minutes)

The simulation shows a warm bulb shifts redness 4.0× more than anaemia does, and that the card cancels it
*in theory*. This experiment measures it with a real phone. It is the "science fair" half of the project.
Report whatever you get, even if the card works less well than the simulation.

## You need

- Your phone, the printed card (matte paper), a laptop running PaleCheck.
- A **red target** that doesn't change: a red sticker, a swatch of red cloth, or a lipstick smear on white paper.
  (Use a fixed object first: your own eyelid changes with blood flow and how you pull it.)
- Three or more lights: window daylight (no direct sun), a tube light, a yellow bulb. Optional: phone torch, white LED.

## Steps

1. Put the card and the red target side by side on a table. Don't move them for the whole experiment.
2. Turn on only one light. Hold the phone in the same position (tape a mark on the table).
3. Take **3 photos** under that light. Don't touch the camera settings.
4. Repeat for every light.
5. Open https://palecheck.zetalabs.in/lab on the phone (or copy the photos to a laptop). Press **Gallery**, load a
   photo; PaleCheck finds the card (tap it with *Card* if not). Tap the red target with *Smart select*, type the
   light's name, press **Add shot**. Do all photos.
6. Press **Export CSV**. Screenshot the Results section for your video and Devpost.

## Optional: your own eyelid

Repeat steps 2–5 with the card next to your eye under 2–3 lights (someone else takes the photos).
Only photograph yourself, or family members who agree.

## What to report

| Measure | Where |
|---|---|
| Redness spread without the card (a* units and × the anaemia gap of 3.6) | Lighting Lab stats |
| Redness spread with the card | Lighting Lab stats |
| % of lighting error removed | Lighting Lab stats |
| Phone model, lights used, number of photos | Your notes |

If the card removes less than the simulation (likely; phones apply their own auto white balance and tone curves),
say so, and give the reason. That honesty is part of the project's story.
